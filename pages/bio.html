🎷My Story

From Steel Mill to Soul Power

You know that saying "steel sharpens steel"? Well honey, I lived it—literally. At 18 years old, I became the first female crane operator in Ohio's steel mills, breaking barriers before I ever stepped on a stage.

Growing up in 1980s Ohio was like living in the epicenter of a musical earthquake. The Staple Singers, Blues legends, R&B queens, Funk masters—that sound was in the air we breathed. I soaked it all up like a sponge, letting it become part of my DNA. My mama used to say I was born with rhythm in my bones and fire in my belly, and she wasn't wrong.

People tell me my voice sounds like Chaka Khan meets Phyllis Hyman—and honestly? That's the best compliment I could ever receive. Those women taught me that power doesn't mean you sacrifice emotion, and emotion doesn't mean you sacrifice technique. You give it all, every single note, every single time.

From opening for Freddie Jackson and recording with members of jazz legends Pieces of A Dream, I've been blessed to share stages with incredible artists. But here's what I've learned: it doesn't matter if you're at a prestigious gala or a local jazz club—when that music hits and you connect with the audience, that's where the magic happens.

These days, I'm working with the legendary Norman Connors, crafting a sound that honors where we came from while pushing soul music somewhere new. But you know what? What really matters is when someone comes up after a show with tears in their eyes, telling me the music touched their soul. That's why I do this.

"I sing because I am human.™ The breath in a note, the way a phrase breaks when the emotion is real, the electricity between a performer and an audience—that cannot be coded. That is the point."

— L.A. Young

The Phyllis Hyman Experience

Let me tell you about Ms. Phyllis. When I first heard her voice as a young girl, something shifted in me. It wasn't just her incredible range or her technical mastery—it was the way she could make you feel every word, every emotion, like she was sitting right next to you, telling you her story.

My Phyllis Hyman tribute isn't just a performance—it's a spiritual connection, a love letter to a woman who changed my life.

I spent years studying her voice, her phrasing, the way she could break your heart with a single note and then lift you back up with the next. When I bring "Ms. Phyllis" back to life on stage, I do it with authenticity, reverence, and a whole lot of soul. People tell me they close their eyes and she's right there in the room with us. That's the highest honor I could ever receive.
