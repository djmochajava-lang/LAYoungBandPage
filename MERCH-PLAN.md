## Part 5: Design Direction Brief

*Use this to brief a designer, or as guidance if creating designs yourself.*

### The L.A. Young Design System

**Primary Typeface:** Playfair Display (serif) — for "L.A. Young" wordmark and all headings
**Secondary Typeface:** A flowing script (Caveat or similar) — for taglines and quotes
**Accent Typeface:** Special Elite (typewriter) — for rubber stamp / credential badges

**Primary Color:** Gold metallic (#ffd700, PMS 871 for metallic ink)
**On Color:** Black (#0a0a0a)
**Accent:** Red (#e63946) — sparingly, for limited editions only
**Neutral:** Cream/Off-white (#f9f6f0) — for vintage/heritage line

### Core Brand Assets to Create

1. **The Wordmark** — "L.A. Young" in custom-refined Playfair Display. Horizontal. Works from 1" to 3 feet. This is the #1 priority.

2. **The Monogram** — "LAY" interlocked or a gold crown/microphone icon. Compact, square/circular. For hats, small placements, favicons.

3. **The Emblem** — Circular badge: "L.A. YOUNG" arched at top, "SOUL • JAZZ • BLUES" at bottom, monogram/icon in center. The Swiss Army knife of designs — works on everything.

4. **"Soul in Full Color" lockup** — The tagline set in its own distinctive treatment, ready to use independently.

5. **"Soul Fam" badge** — Community emblem for fan merch.

### Design Do's and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Use generous negative space — let designs breathe
- Keep to 1-2 colors per design (gold + white or gold only on black)
- Make the garment feel like fashion first, merch second
- Use gold metallic/foil for the flagship products
- Test designs on actual garment mockups, not flat rectangles

**DON'T:**
- Cram text, dates, URLs, and graphics into one design
- Use raw unedited photos on apparel (stylize first)
- Use more than 3 colors on any single product
- Use distressed/grunge fonts (wrong genre association)
- Make designs that only make sense if you know who L.A. Young is

### The "Stranger Test"
Every design must pass this: **Would someone who has never heard of L.A. Young still want to wear/own this?** If yes, you have a winner. If no, it's fan-only merch (which sells less and gets worn less).

## Part 6: Marketing the Merch

### Launch Announcement Strategy

**Email (via Listmonk — already integrated on the site):**

- Add merch links to the show format cards ("Get the look")

**Social Media:**
- Behind-the-scenes of merch creation (unboxing samples, design process)
- "Fan of the week" reposting fans wearing merch
- Style photos of L.A. Young wearing the merch at rehearsals, backstage, daily life

### Concert Merch Strategy

Even though Printful is online-only, you can:
1. Order samples/inventory at cost for live shows (Printful ships to you in bulk at base cost)
2. Set up a merch table with physical inventory at concerts
3. Display QR codes at the merch table linking to the online store for sizes/styles not in stock
4. Offer show-exclusive designs only available at that venue (order a small run of 25-50 per show)
